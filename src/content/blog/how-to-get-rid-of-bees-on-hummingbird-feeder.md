## Why Bees Are Attracted

Understanding why bees are drawn to your hummingbird feeder is key to keeping them away. Bees are naturally attracted to sources of sugar and bright colors, which can make your feeder an irresistible buffet. By knowing what specifically lures them in, you can make smarter choices to protect your feeder for hummingbirds only.

### Sugar Water Appeal

Bees love sugar just as much as hummingbirds do. The sweet nectar you prepare for hummingbirds tastes like a perfect treat to them.

If your feeder contains a high sugar concentration, it becomes even more tempting. Bees are excellent at detecting sugary liquids from a distance, especially when the sugar water is fresh and sweet.

Have you ever noticed bees swarming right after you refill your feeder? That’s because fresh sugar water signals an easy meal to them.

### Feeder Design Factors

The design of your feeder can either invite bees or keep them away. Bright red parts on feeders attract hummingbirds, but bees also notice these colors.

Wide feeding ports or open designs give bees easy access to the sugar water. Narrower ports or bee-proof guards help limit their entry.

Think about your feeder’s shape and size—smaller, enclosed feeding holes make it harder for bees to land and drink.

## Choosing The Right Feeder

Choosing the right feeder can make a big difference in keeping bees away from your hummingbird feeder. It’s not just about picking any feeder off the shelf. The design, materials, and colors all play a role in making your feeder less attractive to bees.

### Bee-resistant Features

Look for feeders designed specifically to keep bees out. Some models have tiny feeding ports that are just the right size for a hummingbird’s beak but too small for bees to access the nectar.

Feeders with built-in bee guards or ant moats can also help reduce bee visits. These barriers block bees from reaching the nectar while still allowing hummingbirds to feed comfortably.

Have you noticed how a simple mesh or netting can make a huge difference? Adding a mesh cover or choosing a feeder with a built-in guard can significantly reduce the number of bees at your feeder.

### Material And Color Choices

The material of your feeder can affect how attractive it is to bees. Glass feeders tend to stay cleaner longer and don’t hold odors that attract bees as much as plastic feeders do.

When it comes to color, red is your best choice. Hummingbirds are attracted to red, while bees are less interested in it. Avoid yellow or orange parts on the feeder, as these colors tend to draw in bees.

Consider feeders with bright red bases and flowers, and plain, transparent reservoirs. This combination can help you enjoy watching hummingbirds without the constant battle against bees.

## Placement Strategies

Placement strategies play a crucial role in keeping bees away from your hummingbird feeder. By carefully choosing where and how you hang your feeder, you can make it less attractive to bees while still welcoming hummingbirds. Small changes in location and positioning can have a big impact on your feeder’s visitors.

### Location Tips

Pick a spot away from flowering plants that attract bees. Bees naturally gravitate towards areas rich in nectar, so placing your feeder far from gardens or blooming shrubs helps reduce unwanted guests.

Try hanging your feeder near trees or bushes that hummingbirds prefer but are less appealing to bees. You might notice that bees avoid shaded, cooler areas, giving you a natural advantage.

Have you noticed that bees often swarm around feeders near doorways or patios? Moving your feeder to a quieter corner of your yard can reduce bee traffic and give hummingbirds a peaceful feeding spot.

### Height And Shade Considerations

Hanging your feeder at about 5 to 6 feet high can keep it out of reach for many bees. This height is perfect for hummingbirds but less convenient for ground-level bee activity.

Shade can help too. Bees love warm, sunny spots where flowers thrive. Placing your feeder in partial shade can cool the nectar and make the feeder less attractive to bees.

Think about the direction of sunlight during the day. A spot that gets morning sun but afternoon shade often works best. This balance helps keep nectar fresh and discourages bee visits.

## Maintaining The Feeder

Maintaining the hummingbird feeder helps keep bees away and keeps birds safe. Clean feeders and fresh nectar stop bees from invading your feeding area. Consistent care ensures hummingbirds visit often without competition from bees.

### Regular Cleaning

Clean the feeder every three days. Use warm water and mild soap to remove sticky residue. Rinse well to avoid soap taste that can harm hummingbirds. Check all feeder parts, including ports and perches, for dirt or mold. Clean thoroughly to prevent bacteria buildup, which attracts bees and other insects.

### Fresh Nectar Preparation

Make nectar with four parts water and one part sugar. Boil water to dissolve sugar, then cool before filling the feeder. Avoid honey or artificial sweeteners; they spoil quickly and attract bees. Replace nectar every two to three days. Fresh nectar stops fermentation and keeps bees from swarming your feeder.

## Natural Deterrents

Keeping bees away from your hummingbird feeder doesn't mean you have to use harsh chemicals or complicated traps. Natural deterrents offer a gentle way to protect your feeder while keeping the environment safe. These solutions work by making your feeder less attractive to bees without harming them, allowing hummingbirds to enjoy the nectar peacefully.

### Essential Oils

Certain essential oils can repel bees effectively without disturbing hummingbirds. Peppermint, citronella, and eucalyptus oils are popular choices because bees dislike their strong scents.

You can create a natural spray by mixing a few drops of essential oil with water in a spray bottle. Spray this mixture around the feeder's base and hanging area, but avoid spraying directly on the feeder to keep the nectar safe for hummingbirds.

Have you noticed how a simple peppermint scent can keep pests at bay in your kitchen? The same principle works outdoors. Experiment with different oils to find which one works best in your garden.

### Herbs And Plants

Planting herbs and flowers that bees tend to avoid near your hummingbird feeder can serve as a natural barrier. Basil, mint, and marigold are great examples that produce scents bees dislike.

Arrange these plants around the feeder to create a protective zone. Not only do they repel bees, but they also add beauty and fragrance to your garden.

Think about your garden’s layout: Could adding a few pots of fresh mint near your feeder reduce bee visits? The added bonus is you get fresh herbs for your kitchen too.

## Homemade Remedies

Homemade remedies offer safe and simple ways to keep bees away from hummingbird feeders. These natural solutions protect the birds while discouraging unwanted visitors. Using common household items can make your feeder less attractive to bees without harming them.

### Vinegar Sprays

Vinegar sprays act as a natural bee repellent. Mix equal parts of water and white vinegar in a spray bottle. Spray around the feeder base and nearby surfaces. Avoid spraying directly on the feeder or hummingbirds. The smell of vinegar helps keep bees at a distance. Reapply the spray every few days for best results.

### Cucumber Peels

Cucumber peels are a gentle way to deter bees. Place fresh cucumber peels near the hummingbird feeder. Bees dislike the scent and will avoid the area. Change the peels every two days to keep the smell fresh. This method is safe for hummingbirds and easy to maintain.

  


## Using Bee Guards

Using bee guards is a simple and effective way to keep bees away from your hummingbird feeder. These small devices fit over the feeding ports and create a barrier that only allows hummingbirds to access the nectar. This protects your feeder from unwanted visitors without harming the bees.

### Installation Tips

Installing bee guards is straightforward, but a few tips can make the process easier. First, make sure your feeder is clean before attaching the guards to prevent sticky residue from trapping bees. Align the guards carefully over each feeding hole to ensure a snug fit.

If your feeder doesn’t come with pre-made holes for bee guards, you can gently modify the guard or feeder to fit. Use a soft cloth to wipe the area afterward to keep it clean and inviting for hummingbirds. Remember, a well-installed guard won’t block the birds but will keep the bees out.

### Effectiveness

Bee guards work best when combined with regular feeder maintenance. They significantly reduce the number of bees around your feeder by limiting access to the nectar. However, they don’t eliminate bees entirely since these insects are attracted to the feeder’s scent.

From my experience, feeders with bee guards need less frequent cleaning because fewer bees mean less spillage and stickiness. Have you noticed how fewer bees make watching your hummingbirds more enjoyable? Bee guards keep the focus on your feathered visitors, not the buzzing intruders.

## Alternative Feeding Options

Bees can be persistent visitors at your hummingbird feeder, but you don’t have to choose between losing your feeder to them or giving up on feeding hummingbirds. Alternative feeding options offer practical ways to share the nectar without constant battles. These solutions let you enjoy watching hummingbirds while managing the bee traffic effectively.

### Multiple Feeders

Setting up multiple feeders can reduce competition and keep bees away from your main hummingbird feeder. Place the feeders at different spots around your yard, ideally spaced at least 10 feet apart.

This separation confuses bees and spreads out their activity. You might notice that bees prefer one feeder, leaving the others mostly for hummingbirds. Have you tried moving your feeders around to see which locations attract fewer bees?

### Separate Bee Feeders

Providing a dedicated feeder just for bees can be surprisingly effective. Use a shallow dish or a bee feeder filled with a sugar water mix similar to your hummingbird nectar, but place it far from your hummingbird feeders.

This keeps bees busy and less interested in the hummingbird feeders. Remember to clean and refill the bee feeder regularly to prevent mold and fermentation. Have you considered giving bees their own nectar spot to keep your hummingbirds happy?

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How Can I Keep Bees Away From My Hummingbird Feeder?

Use bee guards or feeders with bee-proof ports. Place feeders in shaded areas. Clean spills promptly to avoid attracting bees.

### Why Do Bees Swarm Around Hummingbird Feeders?

Bees are attracted to the sweet nectar in feeders. They look for easy sugar sources, so feeders can attract them if not managed.

### What Is The Best Nectar Ratio To Deter Bees?

Use a 1:4 ratio of sugar to water. Bees prefer sweeter nectar, so diluting it helps reduce bee visits without harming hummingbirds.

### Can Natural Repellents Keep Bees Off Feeders?

Yes, natural repellents like peppermint oil can deter bees. Dab a small amount near the feeder but avoid contaminating the nectar.
