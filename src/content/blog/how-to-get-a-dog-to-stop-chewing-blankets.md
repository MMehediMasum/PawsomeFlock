## Reasons Dogs Chew Blankets

Understanding why your dog chews blankets is key to stopping the behavior. Dogs don’t chew blankets just to be naughty—they have real reasons behind this habit. Identifying these reasons helps you address the root cause and guide your dog towards better habits.

### Teething And Puppy Behavior

Puppies often chew blankets because they’re teething. Their gums feel sore, and chewing eases the discomfort. If you have a young dog, expect some chewing as a natural part of growing up.

You can help by giving your puppy safe chew toys. This redirects their need to chew away from your blankets. Have you noticed your puppy chewing more when they’re restless or irritable?

### Boredom And Anxiety

Dogs left alone for long periods may chew blankets out of boredom or anxiety. Chewing becomes a way to relieve stress or pass the time. If your dog shows signs of restlessness or pacing, boredom might be the trigger.

Try adding interactive toys or scheduling more playtime to keep your dog engaged. Sometimes, a simple walk or game of fetch can reduce the urge to chew. What activities does your dog enjoy that could replace blanket chewing?

### Seeking Attention

Blanket chewing can be your dog’s way of getting your attention. Even negative attention, like scolding, feels better than being ignored. If your dog chews only when you’re nearby, they might be asking for interaction.

Try giving your dog positive attention when they behave well instead of reacting to chewing. Reward calm behavior with treats or affection. How often do you catch your dog chewing blankets just when you’re about to leave or come home?

### Exploring With Mouth

Dogs explore the world using their mouths. Chewing blankets is a way to investigate textures and smells. This is especially common in younger dogs who are still learning about their environment.

Offer a variety of textures in chew toys to satisfy this curiosity. Rotate toys regularly to keep your dog interested. Have you noticed your dog’s preference for certain blanket materials or scents?

## Preventing Blanket Chewing

Stopping your dog from chewing blankets starts with preventing the behavior before it becomes a habit. Understanding what drives your dog to chew is key to finding effective solutions. You can create a positive environment that keeps your dog engaged and satisfied, reducing the urge to target your blankets.

### Providing Chew Toys

Offer a variety of chew toys that match your dog’s size and chewing strength. Toys with different textures keep your dog interested and satisfy their natural urge to chew. Rotate the toys regularly to keep them fresh and exciting.

Have you noticed your dog ignoring toys in favor of blankets? Sometimes, it’s about finding the right type—like rubber toys for aggressive chewers or soft plush toys for gentler nibblers. Keep a stash of toys handy to redirect their attention quickly.

### Creating A Comfortable Space

Make your dog’s resting area inviting and safe. Use their own blankets or bedding that you don’t mind if they chew on, so they don’t seek out your favorite blankets. Adding a cozy bed and some personal toys can reduce anxiety-driven chewing.

Think about how your dog feels when you leave the room. Do they have a quiet, secure spot to relax? Sometimes, chewing is a sign of stress or boredom. Giving them a dedicated space can help them feel calm and less likely to chew destructively.

### Exercise And Mental Stimulation

Increase your dog’s daily physical activity to burn off excess energy. A tired dog is less likely to chew out of boredom or frustration. Include walks, playtime, and training sessions to keep their mind sharp and body active.

Have you tried puzzle feeders or training games? These challenge your dog’s brain and keep them occupied. Mental stimulation is just as important as physical exercise when it comes to reducing unwanted chewing.

### Using Deterrent Sprays

Apply safe, bitter-tasting sprays to blankets to discourage chewing. These sprays create an unpleasant taste your dog associates with the item. Be consistent with application, especially on blankets they target most often.

Remember, deterrent sprays work best when combined with positive reinforcement. Praise your dog when they chew on appropriate items instead. This way, you teach them what’s acceptable without causing fear or confusion.

  


## Training Techniques

Training your dog to stop chewing blankets requires patience and clear communication. The right techniques help your dog understand what behavior is acceptable without causing frustration for either of you. These training methods focus on guiding your dog gently while reinforcing positive habits.

### Redirecting Behavior

When you catch your dog chewing a blanket, gently take it away and offer a more appropriate item, like a chew toy. This teaches your dog what is okay to chew on without punishment.

Always have a variety of chew toys available. Observe which toys your dog prefers and use those as the main redirection tools.

Ask yourself: does your dog chew out of boredom or anxiety? Addressing these root causes can reduce unwanted chewing significantly.

### Positive Reinforcement

Reward your dog immediately when they choose a toy over a blanket. Use treats, praise, or playtime as positive feedback.

Timing is crucial. The reward must come right after the good behavior to connect the two in your dog’s mind.

Try to catch small successes too, like sniffing a toy instead of chewing the blanket. This encourages progress and builds trust.

### Consistent Commands

Use simple, clear commands like “leave it” or “no chew.” Consistency helps your dog understand exactly what you expect.

Practice these commands regularly, even when your dog isn’t chewing. This builds a strong foundation for obedience.

Consistency also means everyone in your household uses the same commands and rules. Mixed signals confuse your dog.

### Crate Training Benefits

A crate can be a safe space for your dog to relax without access to blankets. This reduces temptation and helps prevent chewing.

Introduce the crate gradually with positive experiences, such as treats and toys. Make it a cozy retreat, not a punishment.

Crate training also helps with building routines and managing your dog’s behavior when you’re not around. Have you tried this approach yet?

## Managing Environment

Managing the environment plays a key role in stopping a dog from chewing blankets. Changing the surroundings helps reduce temptation and guides the dog’s behavior. Setting clear boundaries and offering better options makes learning easier for your pet.

### Limiting Blanket Access

Keep blankets out of your dog’s reach. Store blankets in closed closets or high shelves. Use baby gates or closed doors to block access to rooms with blankets. Reducing access lowers the chance to chew and helps break the habit.

### Safe Alternatives For Chewing

Provide toys designed for chewing to replace blankets. Choose durable, safe chew toys that match your dog’s size and strength. Rotate toys often to keep your dog interested. This helps satisfy the chewing need without damage.

### Supervision And Monitoring

Watch your dog closely during playtime and rest. Catching chewing early lets you redirect your dog to proper toys. Use gentle commands and praise when your dog chooses the right item. Supervision builds good habits and trust.

## When To Seek Professional Help

Knowing when to seek professional help for your dog’s blanket chewing can save you time and stress. Sometimes, chewing is more than just a bad habit—it might signal deeper issues that need expert attention. Recognizing these signs early allows you to address the root cause effectively.

### Persistent Chewing Issues

If your dog continues chewing blankets despite your best efforts to redirect or discourage the behavior, it’s time to consider professional advice. Persistent chewing might indicate boredom, lack of exercise, or an unmet need that you haven’t identified. Have you tried different toys, more playtime, or changing your dog’s routine without success? When nothing seems to work, professionals can offer tailored strategies.

### Signs Of Anxiety Or Stress

Blanket chewing can be a coping mechanism for anxiety or stress in dogs. Look for other signs like pacing, whining, excessive licking, or changes in appetite. If your dog’s chewing spikes during thunderstorms, separation, or new environments, anxiety might be the cause. Addressing these emotional triggers requires more than simple training techniques.

### Consulting A Veterinarian Or Trainer

Veterinarians can rule out medical issues such as dental pain or nutritional deficiencies that might cause chewing. Trainers or behaviorists specialize in modifying difficult behaviors through positive reinforcement and customized plans. Don’t hesitate to reach out—getting expert help early can improve your dog’s well-being and your peace of mind.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Why Does My Dog Chew Blankets Excessively?

Dogs chew blankets due to boredom, anxiety, or teething. It also helps relieve stress and provides comfort. Identifying the cause helps in addressing the behavior effectively.

### How Can I Stop My Dog From Chewing Blankets?

Provide chew toys, increase exercise, and use deterrent sprays. Consistent training and positive reinforcement discourage blanket chewing. Redirect their attention to appropriate items.

### Are Chew Toys Better Than Blankets For Dogs?

Yes, chew toys are safer and designed for chewing. They satisfy your dog’s natural urge and protect household items. Choose durable, non-toxic toys suitable for your dog’s size.

### Can Anxiety Cause Blanket Chewing In Dogs?

Yes, anxiety often leads to destructive chewing. Providing comfort, exercise, and mental stimulation reduces anxiety-related chewing. Consult a vet for severe cases.

## Conclusion

Stopping your dog from chewing blankets takes patience and care. Offer plenty of toys to keep them busy. Teach gentle chewing with positive rewards. Keep blankets out of easy reach to avoid trouble. Watch your dog closely and redirect chewing when needed.

Consistency helps your dog learn good habits faster. Over time, chewing on blankets will become less interesting. Your dog will enjoy safer, happier playtime. Keep trying, and you will see progress soon.
