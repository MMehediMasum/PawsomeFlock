**Are starlings taking over your bird feeders and scaring away the smaller birds you love to watch? You’re not alone.**

These noisy, aggressive birds can quickly turn your peaceful backyard into their own crowded buffet. But don’t worry—there are simple, effective ways you can get rid of starlings and bring balance back to your bird feeding routine. Keep reading to discover easy tips that will help you protect your feeders and welcome the birds you actually want to see.



## Why Starlings Visit Feeders

Understanding why starlings visit your bird feeders is key to managing their presence. These birds are not just random visitors; they are attracted by specific food types and the design of your feeders. Knowing their preferences helps you make better choices to keep them away without harming other birds you want to feed.

### Attraction To Food Types

Starlings love rich, high-energy foods. They are especially drawn to sunflower seeds, cracked corn, and suet. If your feeder offers these, it’s like a buffet invitation to starlings.

Switching to safflower seeds or nyjer can reduce their visits. These foods are less appealing to starlings but still enjoyed by smaller songbirds. Have you noticed how changing your seed type affects the visitors? It’s a simple change that can make a big difference.

### Feeder Design And Placement

Starlings prefer feeders that are easy to access and have wide perches. Tube feeders with small perches or weight-sensitive mechanisms tend to discourage them. Try feeders designed specifically for smaller birds.

Placement matters too. Starlings feel safer near open spaces where they can spot predators easily. Moving feeders closer to dense shrubs or hanging them in more confined spots can reduce starling visits. Could adjusting the location of your feeder change the type of birds you attract?

## Choosing Starling-resistant Feeders

Choosing the right feeder can help keep starlings away from your bird feeder. Certain designs make it harder for starlings to feed. These feeders allow smaller birds to eat peacefully. The focus is on limiting space and weight capacity. This controls which birds can access the seeds.

### Tube Feeders With Small Perches

Tube feeders with small perches limit where birds can land. Starlings need larger perches to hold their weight. Small perches block them but let smaller birds feed. These feeders usually have narrow seed ports too. This design keeps starlings from reaching the seeds.

### Caged Feeders

Caged feeders have a metal cage around the seed area. The openings are just big enough for small birds. Starlings cannot fit through the cage bars. This protects the seed from larger, aggressive birds. It also keeps squirrels and other pests away.

### Weight-sensitive Feeders

Weight-sensitive feeders close access when heavy birds land. Starlings are heavier than most songbirds. The feeder senses their weight and shuts the seed ports. Small birds can feed normally without triggering the mechanism. This feeder type prevents starlings from dominating the food.

  


## Food Choices To Deter Starlings

Choosing the right food at your bird feeders can make a big difference in keeping starlings away. These birds have specific tastes that don’t always match those of other backyard visitors like finches or chickadees. By understanding what seeds starlings avoid, you can create a feeding environment that attracts your favorite birds while discouraging unwanted guests.

### Seeds Starlings Avoid

Starlings prefer large, easy-to-eat seeds like sunflower seeds and cracked corn. However, they tend to avoid certain types of seeds that are less appealing or harder to handle. Knowing which seeds to use can help you keep starlings at bay without starving your local birds.

* **Nyjer (thistle) seeds:**Tiny and slender, these seeds are perfect for finches but not for starlings, who find them difficult to eat.
* **Safflower seeds:**Starlings dislike the bitter taste, while cardinals and grosbeaks enjoy them.
* **Millet:**Preferred by sparrows and juncos, millet is less attractive to starlings.

Have you noticed that some birds flock to your feeder while others avoid it completely? This difference often comes down to seed choice.

### Using Mixed Seed Strategically

Mixing seeds can be a smart way to feed a variety of birds while limiting starlings. But it’s important to balance the mix carefully to reduce waste and keep starlings uninterested.

* Include mostly safflower or nyjer seeds mixed with smaller amounts of sunflower seeds.
* Avoid large amounts of cracked corn or millet, which can attract starlings if not managed well.
* Use feeders designed for specific seed types to limit access to starlings, such as tube feeders with small ports for nyjer seeds.

Experiment with seed blends and observe which birds show up—and which stay away. What if adjusting your seed mix could transform your backyard into a starling-free zone?

## Feeder Placement And Maintenance

Proper feeder placement and regular maintenance reduce starling visits effectively. Starlings prefer easy access and safe resting spots near feeders. Adjusting feeder location and keeping it clean can discourage these birds. Healthy feeding spaces attract desired birds and keep starlings away.

### Positioning Away From Perches

Place feeders far from trees, fences, or rooftops. Starlings use nearby perches to watch and swoop down. Keep feeders at least 10 feet away from any perch spots. Open spaces make it harder for starlings to approach quietly. Use poles or hanging feeders in clear areas. Avoid placing feeders near dense shrubs or ledges.

### Regular Cleaning And Seed Removal

Clean feeders weekly to remove old seeds and debris. Moldy seeds attract starlings and other pests. Remove spilled seeds from the ground daily. Use a brush and mild soap to scrub feeders thoroughly. Rinse well and dry before refilling. Clean feeders discourage starling nesting and feeding. Fresh seeds attract smaller songbirds instead.

## Additional Deterrents For Starlings

Dealing with starlings at your bird feeders can be frustrating, especially when they scare away the smaller birds you want to attract. Beyond changing feed types or locations, adding extra deterrents can significantly improve your chances of keeping starlings at bay. These additional measures work by targeting the starling’s senses and movement patterns, making your feeders less appealing to them.

### Visual Deterrents

Starlings dislike sudden movements and shiny objects that reflect light unpredictably. Hanging reflective tape or old CDs near your feeders can create flashes that startle them. You might also try placing predator decoys like plastic owls or hawks, but remember to move these every few days to keep the starlings from getting used to them.

### Sound Devices

Sound deterrents use noises that make starlings uncomfortable or anxious. Ultrasonic devices emit high-frequency sounds that humans can’t hear but annoy starlings. Alternatively, recorded predator calls or distress sounds can scare them away, but these are best used sparingly to avoid disturbing your own peace.

### Physical Barriers

Sometimes the best way to stop starlings is to block their access physically. Installing cage-style feeders with openings just large enough for small songbirds keeps starlings out. Another option is using weight-sensitive feeders that close under the heavier weight of a starling but open for lighter birds.

## Encouraging Preferred Birds

Encouraging the birds you want at your feeders can naturally discourage starlings from taking over. By making your yard more attractive to preferred species, you create a welcoming environment that keeps the unwanted visitors away. Think about what your favorite birds need and provide it in a way that starlings find less appealing.

### Creating A Bird-friendly Habitat

Focus on planting native shrubs and trees that provide shelter and nesting spots for the birds you want to attract. Species like chickadees and finches love dense bushes, which can keep starlings at bay since they prefer open areas. You can also add birdhouses designed specifically for small songbirds, making your yard more inviting for them.

Have you noticed how certain plants seem to bring in more of your favorite birds? Adding berry-producing plants like serviceberry or elderberry can feed them naturally throughout the year. This habitat setup encourages a balanced ecosystem where starlings struggle to dominate.

### Providing Alternative Food Sources

Offering food choices that target preferred birds can reduce starlings' interest in your feeders. Try using feeders with smaller openings or weight-sensitive perches that only allow smaller birds to feed. You might also scatter seeds like nyjer or safflower, which starlings tend to avoid.

* Use tube feeders with narrow ports for finches and chickadees.
* Scatter seeds on ground trays to attract sparrows and doves instead of starlings.
* Avoid mixing seeds that starlings find irresistible, like cracked corn or millet.

Have you ever tried putting out suet cakes with mesh cages? These cages keep larger birds away but let smaller ones in. Providing the right food in the right way can shift the balance and help your preferred birds thrive.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How Can I Deter Starlings From Bird Feeders?

Use feeders with smaller openings and weight-sensitive perches. Offer safflower seeds, which starlings dislike. Place feeders in open areas to reduce starling access.

### What Bird Feeder Types Prevent Starling Visits?

Tube feeders with small ports and caged feeders limit starling access. Avoid platform feeders, as starlings prefer them. Choose feeders designed for smaller birds.

### Are There Safe Repellents For Starlings At Feeders?

Yes, visual deterrents like shiny tape or predator decoys help. Avoid harmful chemicals. Regularly change deterrents to maintain effectiveness against starlings.

### Can Changing Feeding Times Reduce Starling Problems?

Feeding early morning or late evening may reduce starling visits. Starlings are less active during these times. Adjust feeding schedule to favor smaller birds.

Remember to observe and adjust as needed. Every backyard is different. Protect your feeders and enjoy the variety of birds visiting. Happy birdwatching!
