## Potential Health Risks

No Hide Chews are popular dog treats, but they come with some health risks. Dog owners should know these risks before giving the chew.

Understanding these risks helps keep dogs safe and healthy while enjoying their treats.

### Choking Hazards

No Hide Chews can break into smaller pieces. These pieces can block a dog’s throat or airway. This can cause choking or difficulty breathing.

Small dogs and aggressive chewers have a higher risk. Always watch your dog while chewing No Hide Chews.

* Large pieces can cause choking
* Small pieces may get stuck in the throat
* Supervise dogs during chewing

### Digestive Issues

Swallowing large bits of No Hide Chews can cause stomach upset. These bits may cause blockages in the intestines. This can lead to vomiting, diarrhea, or worse.

Dogs with sensitive stomachs or previous digestive problems should avoid these chews or use with care.

* Possible vomiting or diarrhea
* Risk of intestinal blockage
* Digestive discomfort in sensitive dogs

### Allergic Reactions

Some dogs may be allergic to ingredients in No Hide Chews. Allergic reactions can cause itching, swelling, or breathing problems.

Watch for unusual signs after giving the chew. Consult a vet if you suspect an allergy.

* Itching or skin irritation
* Swelling of face or paws
* Difficulty breathing or coughing

  


## Choosing The Right Size

Choosing the right size of No Hide Chews is very important for your dog's safety. A chew that is too small can cause choking. A chew that is too big may be hard to chew and break teeth.

Picking the correct size depends on your dog's weight and chewing strength. This helps keep your dog safe and happy while chewing.

### Match Size To Your Dog’s Weight

Check the package for size recommendations based on your dog's weight. This guide helps you find the right chew size. Small dogs need smaller chews. Large dogs need bigger chews.

* Small dogs (under 15 lbs): Small chews
* Medium dogs (15-50 lbs): Medium chews
* Large dogs (over 50 lbs): Large chews

### Consider Your Dog’s Chewing Style

Some dogs are gentle chewers. Others are strong chewers. Strong chewers need tougher, larger chews to avoid breaking the treat too fast.

Observe how your dog chews. Choose a chew that lasts but is not too hard to bite.

### Avoid Choking Hazards

Small chews can break into pieces that block your dog’s throat. Choose a size that your dog can hold but cannot swallow whole.

* Pick chews larger than your dog’s mouth opening
* Watch your dog while chewing
* Remove small pieces to prevent choking

### Replace Worn Out Chews

Chews get smaller as your dog bites them. A small chew can be dangerous. Replace chews when they become too small or thin.

Keep an eye on the chew size during playtime. Swap for a new one before it becomes a choking risk.
