## Frequently Asked Questions



### What Dried Fruits Attract The Most Wild Birds?



Dried fruits like raisins, cranberries, and apricots attract many wild birds. They provide natural sugars and nutrients. Birds such as cardinals and finches especially enjoy these fruits. Using blends like Wagner’s Gourmet Nut & Fruit ensures a variety of dried fruits to attract different species.



### Can Dried Fruit Blends Benefit Wild Bird Health?



Yes, dried fruit blends offer essential vitamins and energy for wild birds. Ingredients like dried peaches and cherries support bird metabolism. Nut and fruit mixes like Pennington Pride enhance nutrition while encouraging birds to visit feeders regularly. These blends promote overall bird health and vitality.



### Which Bird Species Prefer Dried Fruit In Their Diet?



Birds like woodpeckers, robins, cardinals, and chickadees prefer dried fruit. Mealworm-infused blends, such as Kaytee’s, attract insect-eating birds too. Offering a mix of dried fruits and nuts appeals to a wide range of wild bird species, increasing feeder visitation.



### How To Store Dried Fruit Bird Food Properly?



Store dried fruit bird food in a cool, dry place. Keep it in resealable, airtight containers to prevent moisture and mold. Products like Traina’s resealable pouches help maintain freshness. Proper storage ensures the food stays nutritious and safe for wild birds.



### Is Dried Fruit Safe For All Wild Birds?



Most dried fruits are safe for wild birds when unsweetened and free from additives. Avoid dried fruits with preservatives or artificial colors. Natural blends like Wild Delight Fruit N' Berry are formulated specifically for bird consumption, ensuring safety and enjoyment for various species.



### How Often Should I Refill Dried Fruit Bird Feeders?



Refill dried fruit bird feeders every few days or when empty. Frequent refills keep food fresh and birds returning regularly. Monitoring consumption helps prevent spoilage. Using larger bags like Audubon Park’s 15 lb blend ensures you have enough supply for consistent feeding.
