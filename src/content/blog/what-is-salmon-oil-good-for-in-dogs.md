## Dosage And Safety Tips

Giving the right amount of salmon oil to your dog is very important. Too little may not bring benefits. Too much can cause health problems. Knowing safe doses and signs of side effects helps keep your dog healthy and happy.

Always start with a small amount. Watch your dog closely for any changes. Consult your vet before adding salmon oil to your pet’s diet, especially if your dog has health issues or takes medicine.

### Recommended Serving Sizes

Serving size depends on your dog’s weight and health. Below is a simple guide for daily doses:

| Dog Weight | Salmon Oil Amount |
| --- | --- |
| Up to 15 lbs | 1/2 teaspoon |
| 16 to 30 lbs | 1 teaspoon |
| 31 to 60 lbs | 1.5 teaspoons |
| Over 60 lbs | 2 teaspoons |

Divide the dose between meals for better digestion. Do not exceed the recommended amount unless your vet advises it.

### Potential Side Effects

Some dogs may have side effects from salmon oil. Watch for these signs:

* Loose stools or diarrhea
* Bad breath or fishy smell
* Itchy skin or redness
* Vomiting
* Excessive bleeding or bruising

Stop using salmon oil if side effects appear. Contact your vet immediately. Always choose high-quality salmon oil made for pets. Poor quality oils may cause more problems.

## Choosing Quality Salmon Oil

Choosing quality salmon oil ensures your dog receives the best health benefits. Poor-quality products may contain harmful substances or lack essential nutrients. Understanding how to pick pure salmon oil protects your pet’s wellbeing. Focus on ingredients and product sourcing to make the right choice.

### Identifying Pure Products

Look for salmon oil labeled as 100% pure or wild-caught. Pure oils do not mix with fillers or artificial additives. Check the ingredient list; it should contain only salmon oil. Transparency from the brand about sourcing and production methods is a good sign. Choose oils that are cold-pressed or extracted without heat to keep nutrients intact.

### Avoiding Contaminants

Salmon can contain mercury and other toxins. High-quality salmon oil undergoes purification to remove these. Look for third-party testing or certifications on the label. Avoid oils with strong fishy smells, as this may indicate spoilage. Store salmon oil in dark, cool places to prevent oxidation and rancidity.
