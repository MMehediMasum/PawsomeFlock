## Hummingbird Species In Texas

Texas hosts several hummingbird species, each with unique colors and behaviors. These tiny birds bring beauty and energy to gardens and parks. Knowing which species visit helps bird watchers enjoy their presence. Hummingbirds in Texas vary by region and season.

### Common Hummingbirds To Spot

* Ruby-throated Hummingbird: The most common in eastern Texas, bright red throat on males.
* Black-chinned Hummingbird: Found in central and western Texas, males have a purple throat band.
* Buff-bellied Hummingbird: Lives mainly in southern Texas, with a pale orange belly.
* Rufous Hummingbird: Known for fiery orange feathers, passes through Texas during migration.
* Calliope Hummingbird: The smallest bird in North America, seen in western Texas during spring.

### Migration Patterns By Species

Ruby-throated Hummingbirds arrive in Texas from March to April. They migrate north for summer breeding. Black-chinned Hummingbirds follow a similar pattern but appear slightly earlier. Buff-bellied Hummingbirds stay year-round in the southern regions. Rufous Hummingbirds travel long distances, arriving in Texas during March and April. Calliope Hummingbirds pass through Texas mainly in early spring as they head north.

## Factors Influencing Arrival

Hummingbirds return to Texas based on several natural triggers. These factors affect when and where they arrive. Understanding these helps bird watchers and nature lovers prepare for their visit.

### Weather Conditions

Temperature plays a crucial role in hummingbird migration. They avoid cold weather and wait for warmer days. Early spring warmth encourages them to start moving south to Texas. Storms or strong winds can delay their arrival. Calm, sunny days help them travel safely. Changes in weather patterns from year to year also shift their timing.

### Food Availability

Hummingbirds need nectar to fuel their long journeys. The blooming of flowers in Texas signals a food source. They tend to arrive when plants like trumpet vine and salvia start to bloom. Fewer flowers mean fewer birds. Gardens and feeders with sugar water attract them early. Food abundance ensures they stay and breed in the area.

## Attracting Hummingbirds

Attracting hummingbirds to your Texas garden brings color and life. These tiny birds love places that offer food and shelter. Creating a welcoming space invites them back each spring. Simple changes can make your yard a hummingbird hotspot.

### Feeder Types And Placement

Choosing the right feeder is important. Look for feeders that are easy to clean and refill. Red feeders attract hummingbirds because they see red well. Avoid feeders with yellow parts as they can attract bees instead.

Place feeders in a shady spot to keep the nectar fresh. Hang them near flowers to create a natural feeding area. Keep feeders about 4 to 6 feet off the ground. Clean feeders weekly to prevent mold and bacteria growth.

### Plant Choices For Nectar

Hummingbirds prefer flowers rich in nectar. Choose bright, tubular flowers that match their long beaks. Native Texas plants are often best for your local hummingbirds.

* Coral Honeysuckle
* Red Salvia
* Butterfly Bush
* Trumpet Vine
* Scarlet Sage

Plant flowers in groups to create larger feeding areas. Blooming plants throughout the season ensure a steady nectar supply. Avoid pesticides to keep the birds safe and healthy.
