Cesar Millan was born in Mexico and moved to the United States as a young man. He started working with dogs early in life. Over time, he became famous for his ability to calm aggressive dogs. ### Training Philosophy

Cesar believes in using calm and assertive energy when training dogs. He teaches that dogs need clear rules and boundaries. Exercise, discipline, and affection are the three main elements he uses. Cesar has worked with many difficult dogs on his show. He helped dogs with aggression, fear, and anxiety issues. One famous moment is when he calmed a very aggressive pit bull. ## Preparing Your Dog For Training

Getting Cesar Millan to train your dog is an exciting goal, but preparing your dog before the training begins is just as important. Proper preparation ensures your dog is ready to learn and respond effectively. It sets the stage for a smoother training experience and better results.

### Health And Behavior Check

Before any training starts, make sure your dog is in good health. A visit to the vet can rule out any medical issues that might affect behavior or learning. Have you noticed any unusual behaviors or physical discomfort in your dog? Addressing these early can prevent setbacks during training.

Behavioral problems like excessive fear or aggression need special attention. Recognizing these issues beforehand helps tailor the training approach. Don’t hesitate to discuss these concerns with Cesar’s team—they focus on both mental and physical wellness.

### Gathering Training History

Collect all information about your dog’s past training. What methods worked? What didn’t? Knowing this helps Cesar Millan understand your dog’s learning style and personality.

Write down commands your dog already knows and any training tools used, like clickers or leashes. Even small details, like how your dog reacts to strangers or other dogs, can change the training plan. Have you kept a journal or notes on your dog’s progress so far? These records become valuable assets.

### Setting Realistic Goals

Think about what you want to achieve with Cesar Millan’s training. Clear, realistic goals will keep you and the trainer focused. Is your priority better leash manners, reducing anxiety, or improving social behavior?

Set goals that match your dog’s age, breed, and temperament. For example, expecting a senior dog to learn complex new commands quickly might be unrealistic. What small wins can you celebrate along the way? Recognizing progress keeps motivation high for both you and your dog.
