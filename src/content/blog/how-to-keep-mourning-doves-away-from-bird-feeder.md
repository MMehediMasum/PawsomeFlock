**Are you tired of mourning doves taking over your bird feeder and scaring away other birds you want to attract? You’re not alone.**

While mourning doves are beautiful, they can be quite persistent and often eat more than their share of birdseed. If you want to keep your feeder full and welcoming to a variety of birds, it’s important to find ways to gently discourage mourning doves without harming them.

You’ll discover simple, effective tips to keep mourning doves away from your feeder—so you can enjoy a more balanced and lively backyard birdwatching experience. Keep reading to learn how to protect your feeder and invite the birds you really want to see.

## Why Mourning Doves Visit Feeders

Mourning doves are common visitors at many bird feeders, and understanding why they come can help you manage their presence better. These gentle birds are drawn by specific food choices and feeder designs that suit their feeding habits. Knowing what attracts them allows you to adjust your feeder setup effectively.

### Preferred Food Types

Mourning doves mainly enjoy small seeds like millet, cracked corn, and sunflower seeds. They prefer seeds that fall on the ground or are easy to pick up from flat surfaces. If your feeder offers these types of seeds, it naturally becomes a hotspot for doves.

Consider this: I once switched from mixed seed to safflower seed only, and noticed fewer doves at the feeder, while other birds still visited. This shows how seed choice can influence which birds show up.

### Feeder Design Attraction

Mourning doves prefer feeders that allow them to perch comfortably and access seeds easily. Platform feeders and ground feeders are their favorites because they provide ample space to land and feed without competition.

If your feeder has small perches or narrow feeding ports, doves might avoid it. Have you thought about using feeders designed to exclude larger birds? This simple change can reduce dove visits while still feeding smaller songbirds.

## Choosing Dove-resistant Feeders

Choosing feeders that doves cannot easily access helps protect your bird feeder. These feeders limit dove access but still allow smaller birds to feed comfortably. The right design keeps mourning doves away without disturbing other wildlife.

### Tube Feeders With Small Perches

Tube feeders with tiny perches make it hard for doves to land. Mourning doves are larger birds and need more space to perch. Small perches prevent them from settling on the feeder.

These feeders attract finches, chickadees, and other small birds. They can easily grab seeds without competition from doves. Tube feeders also help keep seeds dry and fresh.

### Caged Feeders

Caged feeders have a metal cage surrounding the seed area. The cage holes allow small birds to enter but block bigger birds like doves. This design keeps mourning doves from reaching the seeds.

Such feeders work well for sunflower seeds and mixed seed blends. They protect the seed supply and reduce waste from larger birds. Caged feeders also discourage squirrels and other large animals.

## Selecting Seeds To Deter Doves

Selecting the right seeds plays a crucial role in keeping mourning doves away from your bird feeder. These birds have specific preferences that you can use to your advantage. Choosing seeds they don’t like will help you attract other birds while gently discouraging doves from visiting.

### Avoiding Millet And Sunflower

Mourning doves love millet and sunflower seeds. If your feeder is full of these, doves will flock to it quickly. Removing or reducing these seeds can make your feeder less appealing to them.

Instead of filling your feeder with millet and sunflower, try to keep these seeds out entirely. You might notice fewer doves and more variety of other birds like chickadees or finches. Have you ever wondered why some feeders attract so many doves while others don’t? The seed mix is often the key.

### Using Niger And Safflower Seeds

Niger and safflower seeds are less favored by mourning doves. These seeds attract smaller songbirds such as finches, cardinals, and grosbeaks. Using them can help you keep doves at bay while still feeding your feathered friends.

Many bird watchers report that switching to safflower seeds drastically reduced dove visits. Niger seeds, often used in tube feeders, also work well to attract a colorful variety of birds without inviting doves. Are you ready to try this small change that can make a big difference in your backyard bird life?

## Physical Barriers And Deterrents

Physical barriers and deterrents offer a hands-on way to keep mourning doves away from your bird feeder. These tools don’t harm the birds but make it difficult or uncomfortable for them to land or feed. You can use simple items that create movement, block access, or reflect light to discourage these birds from settling in.

### Using Spinners And Reflectors

Spinners and reflectors work by catching the light and creating motion, which unsettles mourning doves. Hanging shiny objects like CDs, aluminum foil strips, or commercial bird spinners near your feeder can make the area less attractive to them.

Place these items where they catch sunlight and spin freely in the breeze. Have you noticed how doves quickly fly away when something suddenly flashes or moves? That’s the effect you want to achieve. Just make sure the reflectors don’t disturb other birds you want to attract.

### Installing Baffles And Screens

Baffles and screens physically block mourning doves from reaching your feeder. You can install a dome-shaped baffle above the feeder pole or attach mesh screens around feeding areas. These barriers prevent doves from landing or accessing the seeds.

Choose materials sturdy enough to withstand weather but easy to clean. Positioning is key—place baffles so they don’t interfere with your favorite smaller birds. Have you ever tried watching a bird feeder only to see doves effortlessly take over? Baffles change that by giving you control over who visits.

  


## Feeding Strategies To Reduce Dove Visits

Managing how and when you feed birds can make a big difference in keeping mourning doves away from your feeder. Adjusting your feeding strategies can help you attract the birds you want while discouraging doves, which often prefer easy access to spilled seeds and consistent food availability.

### Timed Feeding

Offering food only at certain times of the day can limit dove visits. Mourning doves like to feed throughout the day, so restricting feeding to short, predictable periods reduces their opportunities.

Try putting out seed in the early morning and removing it by mid-morning. This approach suits many smaller songbirds that feed actively during those hours but may discourage doves, which tend to linger longer.

Have you noticed your doves hanging around all day? Setting a strict feeding schedule could change their behavior quickly.

### Limiting Seed Spill

Doves are ground feeders and thrive on seeds that fall to the ground. Controlling seed spill can make your feeding area less attractive to them.

Use feeders designed to minimize seed drop, such as tube feeders with seed trays or hopper feeders with well-fitted lids. Placing a tray or mat beneath the feeder can help catch stray seeds for easy cleanup.

Regularly clean up spilled seeds to avoid creating a buffet for doves. Have you tried adjusting your feeder type or cleaning routine to reduce mess? You might find it cuts down dove visits more than expected.

## Alternative Feeding Areas For Doves

Providing alternative feeding areas helps keep mourning doves away from your main bird feeder. It gives them a special place to eat without disturbing other birds. This approach respects their needs and keeps your feeders less crowded.

### Creating Separate Feeding Stations

Set up a feeding station just for mourning doves. Place it away from your main feeder to reduce competition. Use flat trays or ground feeders with seeds doves prefer, like millet or cracked corn.

Choose a quiet spot with some cover nearby. This makes doves feel safe while eating. Clean these stations regularly to keep the area healthy for birds.

### Planting Dove-friendly Vegetation

Grow plants that produce seeds loved by mourning doves. Native grasses and sunflowers attract them naturally. These plants provide food and a natural habitat.

Plant shrubs or low bushes to offer shelter. This encourages doves to stay in these areas instead of your feeders. Using plants creates a peaceful space for doves and other wildlife.

## Maintaining Cleanliness To Discourage Doves

Maintaining cleanliness around your bird feeder plays a crucial role in discouraging mourning doves from settling in. Doves are attracted to easy food sources, especially spilled seeds and leftover debris. By keeping the area tidy, you reduce these attractions and make your feeder less inviting to them.

### Regular Feeder Cleaning

Clean your bird feeder at least once a week to prevent seed buildup and mold growth. Use warm, soapy water and a brush to scrub all surfaces thoroughly. Rinse well and let it dry completely before refilling.

Neglecting this routine can lead to spoiled seeds that attract not just doves but other unwanted pests. You might notice fewer doves visiting when the feeder looks fresh and inviting only to preferred birds.

### Managing Ground Seed

Seeds that fall to the ground are a major lure for mourning doves. Sweep up spilled seeds daily or place a seed tray beneath the feeder to catch excess. This simple habit cuts down on easy meals that doves rely on.

Consider using feeders designed to minimize seed drop. Have you tried feeders with seed catchers or trays? They can make a noticeable difference in keeping the ground clean and doves away.

## Legal And Ethical Considerations

Keeping mourning doves away from your bird feeder requires thoughtful action. Legal and ethical concerns guide how to approach this task. Respect for wildlife and local laws ensures safe and kind treatment of all birds.

### Protecting Native Wildlife

Mourning doves are protected by laws in many areas. These laws prevent harm or harassment. Avoid trapping or injuring them. Use non-harmful methods to deter doves. This keeps the natural balance and protects all species.

Consider the role doves play in the ecosystem. They help spread seeds and support other wildlife. Protecting native birds means supporting a healthy environment for everyone.

### Avoiding Harmful Deterrents

Never use poisons or harmful chemicals to keep doves away. Such methods can hurt other animals and the environment. Loud noises or flashing lights can stress birds but should be used sparingly. Choose gentle deterrents that do not cause injury or fear.

Options like feeder placement and specific seed choices work well. These solutions respect bird welfare and comply with legal rules. Ethical care means keeping feeders safe for all visitors.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How Can I Deter Mourning Doves From My Bird Feeder?

Use feeders with smaller openings to block doves. Place feeders in open areas away from perches. Adding baffles or mesh guards also helps keep doves out effectively.

### What Seeds Do Mourning Doves Prefer At Feeders?

Mourning doves prefer millet, cracked corn, and sunflower seeds. Avoid these or use safflower seeds to discourage them. Choosing the right seed mix can reduce dove visits.

### Are Certain Feeder Types Less Attractive To Mourning Doves?

Tube feeders with small ports limit dove access. Platform feeders attract doves, so avoid using them. Selecting feeder types wisely controls which birds visit.

### Can Physical Barriers Keep Mourning Doves Away?

Yes, installing mesh or wire guards around feeders blocks doves. Baffles on poles prevent doves from perching nearby. Physical barriers are a practical way to deter them.
