## Feeding Guidelines

Feeding your dog the right amount of Blue Dog Food is key to keeping them healthy and happy. Understanding the feeding guidelines helps you avoid overfeeding or underfeeding, both of which can impact your dog's well-being. Below, you’ll find practical tips on portion sizes, how to smoothly transition your dog to Blue Dog Food, and the best ways to store it for freshness.

### Portion Sizes

Blue Dog Food provides a feeding chart on the packaging that matches your dog's weight and activity level. Use this as a starting point, but watch your dog’s body condition closely. If they seem too thin or start gaining excess weight, adjust the portions accordingly.

Remember, active dogs may need more food, while less active or older dogs require smaller amounts. Measuring your dog's meals instead of free-feeding helps maintain portion control and prevents overeating.

### Transitioning Tips

Switch your dog to Blue Dog Food gradually over 7 to 10 days. Start by mixing 25% of Blue Dog Food with 75% of their current food.

Slowly increase the amount of Blue Dog Food while decreasing the old food. This reduces stomach upset and helps your dog get used to the new taste and texture.

Watch for any signs of allergies or digestive issues during the transition. If you notice anything unusual, consult your vet before continuing.

### Storage Advice

Keep Blue Dog Food in a cool, dry place away from sunlight. Proper storage preserves nutrients and prevents spoilage.

Use airtight containers to keep the food fresh and protect it from pests. Avoid storing food in the bag if it’s not resealable, as exposure to air can make it go stale faster.

How often do you check your dog food’s freshness? Regularly rotating stock and using older food first helps ensure your dog always eats fresh meals.
