**Are you tired of seeing honey bees swarm around your hummingbird feeder? It can be frustrating when these busy insects take over, making it hard for the hummingbirds you want to watch to enjoy their food.**

But don’t worry—you can keep honey bees away without harming them or your feathered friends. You’ll discover simple and effective tips to protect your hummingbird feeder and make sure it stays a safe, welcoming spot just for the birds. Keep reading to learn how to enjoy your backyard visitors without the unwanted buzz!



## Why Bees Are Attracted To Feeders

Understanding why honey bees are attracted to hummingbird feeders is key to keeping them away. Bees seek out sources of sugar to fuel their daily activities, and hummingbird feeders often provide a tempting spot. By knowing what draws bees in, you can make smarter choices to protect your feeders for hummingbirds only.

### Nectar Sweetness And Scent

Honey bees are highly sensitive to sweetness. The sugar water in hummingbird feeders mimics natural nectar, making it an irresistible food source.

Bees also rely on scent to find flowers and food. The sugary solution emits a scent that bees quickly learn to associate with food. This scent can attract large numbers of bees from nearby areas.

Have you noticed how bees swarm around feeders soon after you refill them? That’s because fresh nectar or sugar water releases a stronger scent that acts like a beacon. Reducing the sweetness slightly or changing the sugar ratio might make your feeder less inviting to bees without harming hummingbirds.

### Feeder Design And Placement

The shape and color of your feeder can either invite or deter bees. Feeders with wide feeding ports or shallow nectar reservoirs give easy access to bees.

Placing feeders away from flowering plants or bee hives also helps reduce visits. Bees often patrol areas near their nests or rich floral sources, so locating your feeder in a less bee-friendly spot can make a difference.

Consider feeders with bee guards—small plastic barriers that block bee entry but still allow hummingbirds to feed. Combining this with careful placement reduces unwanted visitors and keeps your hummingbird guests happy.

## Choosing Bee-resistant Feeders

Choosing bee-resistant feeders helps protect hummingbirds from aggressive honey bees. These feeders limit bee access while allowing hummingbirds to feed freely. Selecting the right feeder reduces bee visits and keeps feeders clean. It also helps maintain a safe feeding environment for hummingbirds.

### Feeders With Bee Guards

Feeders with bee guards have small mesh or plastic barriers. These barriers block bees but let hummingbirds reach the nectar. The guard stops bees from landing on feeding ports. This design keeps bees away without disturbing hummingbirds.

Bee guards are easy to clean and maintain. They reduce the chance of nectar contamination by bees. Using feeders with bee guards is a simple way to protect your hummingbird feeder.

### Using Bee-proof Feeding Ports

Bee-proof feeding ports are specially shaped to exclude bees. These ports are smaller and fit hummingbird beaks perfectly. Bees cannot fit their tongues into these tight openings. This prevents them from accessing the nectar.

These ports often use soft rubber or silicone materials. They allow hummingbirds to feed quickly and safely. Bee-proof feeding ports make your feeder less attractive to honey bees.

## Placement Strategies To Deter Bees

Placement plays a big role in keeping honey bees away from hummingbird feeders. Bees are attracted to bright colors and sweet smells near flowers. Properly placing your feeder can reduce bee visits and keep hummingbirds happy.

### Location Away From Flowers

Keep feeders far from blooming plants. Bees search for nectar in flowers first. Placing feeders at least 10 feet away from flowers lowers bee activity. Avoid putting feeders near gardens or flower beds. Choose spots with few or no flowers around.

### Height And Shade Considerations

Hang feeders higher, around 5 to 6 feet off the ground. Bees tend to stay closer to the ground near flowers. Place feeders in shaded or partially shaded areas. Bees prefer sunny spots for warming up. Shade cools the nectar, making it less attractive to bees.

## Adjusting Nectar Recipes

Adjusting the nectar recipe in hummingbird feeders helps reduce honey bee visits. Bees and hummingbirds prefer different sugar concentrations. Changing the sugar-to-water ratio can make nectar less attractive to bees while still feeding hummingbirds. Also, avoiding certain additives that attract bees keeps them away. Small changes in the recipe make a big difference.

### Sugar-to-water Ratios

Use a sugar-to-water ratio of 1:5 or 1:6 to discourage bees. Hummingbirds can feed on weaker nectar, but bees prefer stronger sugar solutions. Standard nectar recipes use 1:4, which attracts both birds and bees. Diluting the nectar lowers sugar concentration, making it less appealing to bees.

Try these ratios:

* 1 cup sugar to 5 cups water
* 1 cup sugar to 6 cups water

Mix sugar in hot water until fully dissolved. Let it cool before filling the feeder. This recipe still gives hummingbirds energy without inviting bees.

### Avoiding Additives That Attract Bees

Do not add red dye or fruit juices to the nectar. These ingredients attract bees and other insects. Use clear, natural nectar made from just sugar and water. Avoid artificial sweeteners or honey in the mix. Bees are drawn to strong scents and sweet smells, so keep the nectar simple.

Keep feeders clean to prevent leftover residue that attracts bees. Clean feeders weekly with hot water and mild soap. Rinse well to remove all soap traces. Clean feeders help keep nectar fresh and less inviting to honey bees.

## Physical Barriers And Deterrents

Physical barriers and deterrents help stop honey bees from reaching hummingbird feeders. These methods create obstacles or unpleasant conditions for bees without harming the birds. Using physical solutions keeps feeders clean and available for hummingbirds only.

### Using Mesh Or Screens

Covering hummingbird feeders with fine mesh or screens blocks honey bees. The mesh holes must be small enough to stop bees but large enough for hummingbirds to feed.

* Use nylon or metal mesh with holes around 1/8 inch wide.
* Place the mesh around feeder ports or hang it as a cage around the feeder.
* Check and clean the mesh regularly to keep it effective.

This barrier lets hummingbirds feed easily while keeping bees out. It is a safe and simple way to protect the nectar.

### Natural Bee Repellents Near Feeders

Natural scents can discourage bees from approaching feeders. These repellents do not harm bees but make the area less attractive to them.

* Place small pots of mint, eucalyptus, or citronella near feeders.
* Use clove oil or cinnamon oil on cotton balls around the feeder stand.
* Replace or refresh repellents every few days to keep the scent strong.

These natural smells keep bees away while hummingbirds stay unaffected. They work well alongside physical barriers for better results.

## Maintenance And Cleaning Tips

Keeping honey bees away from your hummingbird feeders starts with proper maintenance and cleaning. Regular attention to your feeder not only keeps it inviting for hummingbirds but also discourages bees from taking over. Clean feeders prevent buildup of sticky nectar, which attracts unwanted insects.

### Regular Feeder Cleaning

Clean your feeder every 2 to 3 days, especially in warm weather. Old nectar becomes sticky and ferments quickly, attracting bees and other pests. Use hot water and a small brush to scrub all feeder parts thoroughly.

Avoid using soap or harsh chemicals, as residues can harm hummingbirds. Rinse well to ensure no cleaning agents remain. You might be surprised how much easier it is to keep bees away simply by maintaining a fresh, clean feeder.

### Preventing Fermentation And Mold

Fermented nectar smells sweet but can be harmful and attracts more bees. Change the nectar every 1 to 2 days to prevent fermentation, especially during hot weather. Mold can also grow in old nectar, which is unhealthy for hummingbirds.

Store extra nectar in the fridge to keep it fresh longer. Consider making smaller batches to avoid leftover nectar sitting too long. Have you noticed how quickly nectar can turn sour on a hot day? This quick turnover can be your best defense against bees.

  


## Alternative Feeding Options

Keeping honey bees away from hummingbird feeders can be tricky, but offering alternative feeding options can help both creatures thrive without conflict. By providing separate food sources for bees, you give hummingbirds the space they need while still supporting the bees' natural foraging habits. This approach not only protects your hummingbird feeders but also encourages a healthy environment for pollinators.

### Separate Feeders For Bees

Setting up a dedicated feeder just for bees can divert their attention away from your hummingbird feeders. Use a shallow dish filled with sugar water diluted to a 1:4 ratio (one part sugar to four parts water), which is perfect for bees but less attractive to hummingbirds.

Place the bee feeder at least 10 feet away from the hummingbird feeder and near flowers or plants bees enjoy. This separation reduces competition and keeps your hummingbird feeder bee-free. Have you noticed how bees quickly find a new source when offered an easier meal?

### Planting Bee-friendly Flowers

Planting flowers that attract bees gives them a natural food source and keeps them busy in your garden. Consider adding lavender, bee balm, or coneflowers, which bees love and that bloom over long periods.

These flowers not only help keep bees away from your feeders but also improve your garden’s health and beauty. Could your garden benefit from a few more bee-friendly blooms to balance nature and your hummingbird’s needs?
