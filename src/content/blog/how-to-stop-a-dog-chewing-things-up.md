**Is your dog turning your favorite shoes, furniture, or important papers into a pile of chewed-up mess? You’re not alone, and the good news is—there’s a way to stop this behavior.**

Understanding why your dog chews and learning simple, effective strategies can save your belongings and make life at home much more peaceful. Keep reading, because by the end of this article, you’ll have clear steps to protect your things and help your dog feel happier and more content.

## Reasons Dogs Chew

Dogs chew for many reasons. It is a natural behavior for them. Understanding why they chew helps in managing it.

Chewing can be caused by teething, boredom, anxiety, or curiosity. Each reason needs different solutions.

### Teething In Puppies

Puppies chew a lot when they are teething. Their gums hurt and chewing helps soothe them.

During this time, puppies need safe items to chew. This prevents damage to your belongings.

* Provide teething toys
* Use frozen treats for soothing
* Keep shoes and cords out of reach

### Boredom And Anxiety

Dogs chew when bored or anxious. It is a way to pass time and release stress.

Ensuring your dog gets enough exercise can help. Mental stimulation is also important.

* Take your dog for daily walks
* Play interactive games
* Provide puzzle toys for mental exercise

### Seeking Attention

Dogs might chew to get your attention. They learn that chewing makes you react.

To stop this, ignore the behavior. Reward them when they play with their own toys.

* Ignore unwanted chewing
* Praise for using toys
* Spend quality time with your dog

### Exploring The Environment

Dogs explore the world with their mouths. Chewing helps them learn about their surroundings.

Provide safe items for exploration. This keeps them away from harmful objects.

* Offer a variety of chew toys
* Rotate toys to keep interest
* Watch for dangerous objects

## Identifying Chewing Triggers

Dogs chew for many reasons. Finding what causes chewing helps stop the behavior.

Look closely at when and what your dog chews. This shows the chewing triggers.

### Common Household Items

Dogs often chew things they find around the house. These items may smell like you.

Common items include shoes, remote controls, pillows, and furniture legs. These items are easy to grab.

* Shoes and slippers
* Electrical cords
* Books and magazines
* Clothing like socks and shirts
* Plastic containers

### Time Of Day Patterns

Dogs may chew more at certain times. Watch your dog’s daily routine closely.

Chewing often happens when dogs are bored or tired. Early morning and late evening are common times.

* Morning: dogs may chew after waking up
* Afternoon: boredom may increase chewing
* Evening: chewing can happen before bedtime
* When left alone: anxiety can cause chewing

### Emotional States

Dogs chew to express feelings. Stress and excitement can cause chewing.

Chewing helps dogs calm down. It also relieves frustration or loneliness.

* Stress: new environment or loud noises
* Excitement: when greeting people or playing
* Loneliness: chewing when left alone
* Frustration: lack of exercise or attention

  


## Training Techniques To Prevent Chewing

Dogs often chew things because they are curious or bored. Training can help stop this behavior before it causes damage.

Using clear training methods teaches your dog what is okay to chew. This helps keep your home safe and your dog happy.

### Redirecting To Appropriate Toys

Give your dog toys that are safe and fun to chew. When you see your dog chewing on something wrong, gently take it away.

Offer a chew toy instead. This helps your dog learn what is okay to bite and what is not.

* Keep a variety of chew toys available
* Choose toys based on your dog’s size
* Use toys that are durable and safe
* Replace toys when they get worn out

### Using Positive Reinforcement

Praise your dog when it chews on toys instead of furniture or shoes. Use treats or kind words to reward good behavior.

This makes your dog want to repeat the good actions. Avoid yelling or punishment, as it can cause fear or confusion.

* Give a treat immediately after good chewing
* Use a happy voice to praise your dog
* Repeat rewards often to build good habits
* Be patient while your dog learns

### Establishing Boundaries

Set clear rules about what your dog can and cannot chew. Keep tempting items out of reach to avoid confusion.

Use baby gates or closed doors to limit access to certain rooms. This helps your dog understand limits and stay safe.

* Remove shoes, cables, and small objects
* Block off areas with fragile items
* Use a crate or playpen when unsupervised
* Teach your dog which rooms are off-limits

### Consistency In Commands

Use the same words and tone every time you tell your dog "no" or "leave it." Consistency helps dogs learn faster.

Everyone in the home should follow the same rules. Mixed messages can confuse your dog and slow down training.

* Choose simple commands like “no” or “leave it”
* Use the command every time unwanted chewing starts
* Reward when your dog stops chewing after the command
* Make sure all family members use the same commands

## Managing The Environment

Stopping a dog from chewing things often starts with managing their environment. Dogs chew to explore, relieve boredom, or ease stress. Changing their surroundings helps prevent unwanted chewing.

By controlling what your dog can reach and giving them better options, you can protect your belongings and keep your dog happy.

### Removing Tempting Objects

Keep items your dog likes to chew out of reach. This reduces chances they will destroy your things. Common tempting objects include shoes, wires, and children’s toys.

Put these items in closed cabinets or on high shelves. This makes it harder for your dog to grab them.

* Store shoes in closets
* Hide electrical cords safely
* Keep small toys off the floor
* Secure trash cans with lids

### Creating A Safe Space

Give your dog a special area where they feel safe and relaxed. This space should have their bed, toys, and water. It helps reduce stress and unwanted chewing.

Use baby gates or crates to limit access to rooms with many chewable items. This helps your dog learn boundaries.

* Choose a quiet corner
* Provide soft bedding
* Place favorite chew toys nearby
* Use a crate if your dog is crate-trained

### Providing Mental Stimulation

Bored dogs chew more. Give your dog mental challenges to keep their mind busy. This lowers the chance they will chew things they should not.

Toys that hide treats or puzzles can keep dogs entertained for a long time. Regular training sessions also provide good mental exercise.

* Use treat-dispensing toys
* Offer puzzle feeders
* Teach new commands daily
* Rotate toys to keep interest

## Using Deterrents Safely

Dogs often chew things to explore or relieve boredom. Using deterrents can help stop this behavior. It is important to use deterrents safely to protect your dog and your belongings.

Choose deterrents that are harmless and avoid causing stress. Always watch your dog’s reaction and adjust if needed.

### Bitter Sprays

Bitter sprays taste unpleasant to dogs. They discourage chewing on furniture, shoes, or other items. These sprays are usually safe but should be used carefully.

Test the spray on a small area to check for damage. Avoid spraying near your dog’s face or food. Reapply as needed to keep the taste strong.

### Noise Deterrents

Noise deterrents startle dogs when they chew. Common types include cans with coins or ultrasonic devices. They help interrupt the chewing habit safely.

Use noise deterrents only when you see your dog chewing. Avoid constant noise, which can scare or stress your pet. Always pair noise deterrents with positive training.

### Physical Barriers

Physical barriers keep dogs away from objects they like to chew. Barriers can be gates, boxes, or covers. They stop access without harming your dog.

Make sure barriers are secure and safe. Do not use anything sharp or heavy that could hurt your dog. Combine barriers with training for best results.

## Exercise And Engagement

Dogs often chew things when they feel bored or have extra energy. Giving your dog enough exercise and mental engagement helps reduce chewing behavior. Keeping your dog busy makes them happier and less likely to destroy items around your home.

Exercise and engagement work together to keep your dog calm and focused. Use walks, playtime, toys, and training to meet their physical and mental needs.

### Daily Walks And Playtime

Daily walks help your dog burn off energy and stay healthy. A tired dog is less likely to chew on furniture or shoes. Walks also let your dog explore and enjoy new smells.

Playtime with your dog builds a strong bond. Playing fetch or tug-of-war helps your dog use energy in a fun way. Regular play reduces anxiety and boredom.

* Take your dog for walks at least once a day
* Include play sessions after walks for extra energy release
* Use safe toys like balls or ropes for playtime

### Interactive Toys

Interactive toys keep your dog busy and challenge their mind. Puzzle feeders and treat-dispensing toys make your dog work for food. This slows eating and stops boredom chewing.

Rotating toys keeps them interesting. Give different toys on different days to keep your dog curious and engaged.

* Choose toys that fit your dog’s size and chewing style
* Use puzzle toys to make your dog think
* Swap toys regularly to keep your dog interested

### Training Sessions

Training sessions teach your dog good behavior and provide mental exercise. Short, frequent training helps focus your dog’s attention. Training also builds trust and communication.

Use positive rewards like treats or praise. Training your dog to chew only on their toys helps stop unwanted chewing.

* Keep training sessions short, about 5 to 10 minutes
* Teach commands like “leave it” and “drop it”
* Reward good behavior immediately with treats or praise

## When To Seek Professional Help

Dogs often chew things as part of their natural behavior. Sometimes, this chewing can cause damage or become a problem. Knowing when to get help from a professional is important for your dog's health and your home.

If your dog's chewing does not improve with basic training or if it causes safety concerns, it is time to seek expert advice. Professionals can identify deeper issues and offer effective solutions.

### Persistent Chewing Issues

Some dogs keep chewing even after you try different training methods. This can be a sign of stress, boredom, or medical problems. Persistent chewing can damage your belongings and hurt your dog.

If your dog chews furniture, shoes, or other objects for weeks or months, you should ask a professional for help. Early action can stop serious damage and keep your dog safe.

### Behavioral Assessments

Professionals can do behavioral assessments to understand why your dog chews. They watch your dog’s habits and environment carefully. This helps find the root cause of the chewing problem.

After the assessment, they suggest a plan to change your dog’s behavior. This plan might include training, exercise, or changes to your dog’s daily routine.

### Working With Trainers Or Vets

Trainers and vets have special skills to help dogs stop chewing. Trainers focus on teaching your dog new habits. Vets check if health issues cause the chewing.

They work together to give your dog the best care. Follow their advice carefully. This teamwork helps your dog feel better and stop chewing unwanted items.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Why Does My Dog Chew Things Destructively?

Dogs chew to relieve stress, boredom, or teething pain. It’s a natural behavior that needs proper outlets and training.

### How Can I Stop My Dog From Chewing Furniture?

Provide chew toys, supervise your dog, and use deterrent sprays. Consistent training and exercise reduce destructive chewing habits.

### What Are The Best Chew Toys For Dogs?

Durable rubber, nylon, and rope toys are ideal. They satisfy chewing urges and help maintain dental health.

### Can Crate Training Help Reduce Chewing?

Yes, crate training limits unsupervised access to household items. It provides a safe space and supports behavior control.

## Conclusion

Stopping a dog from chewing takes time and patience. Offer your dog safe toys to chew instead. Watch your dog closely and gently correct bad behavior. Praise good behavior to encourage your dog. Keep tempting items out of reach to avoid trouble.

Training and care help your dog feel happy and calm. Remember, your dog learns best with love and consistency. Keep trying, and you will see progress. A happy dog means a happy home.
