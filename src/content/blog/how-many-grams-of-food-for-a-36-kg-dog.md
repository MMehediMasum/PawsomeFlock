## Daily Calorie Needs

Understanding your 36 kg dog's daily calorie needs is crucial for keeping them healthy and energetic. Feeding too much can lead to weight gain and health issues, while too little can cause nutrient deficiencies and fatigue. Knowing the right amount helps you support their activity level and overall well-being.

### Factors Affecting Calorie Requirements

Several factors affect how many calories your dog needs each day. Age plays a big role: puppies and young dogs burn more energy growing, while older dogs may require fewer calories.

Activity level matters a lot. A highly active dog will need more calories than a mostly sedentary one. Even the environment can influence calorie needs—dogs in colder climates might burn more energy staying warm.

Health conditions and whether your dog is neutered or spayed also impact calorie demands. Have you noticed your dog’s appetite change with the seasons or after a change in routine? That’s a clue their calorie needs might be shifting.

### Calculating Calories For A 36 Kg Dog

To estimate calories, start with the Resting Energy Requirement (RER), which is the amount of energy your dog needs at rest. Use this formula:

| Calculation | Formula |
| --- | --- |
| RER (kcal/day) | 70 × (body weight in kg)0.75 |

For a 36 kg dog, the RER is approximately 1,170 kcal/day.

Next, adjust based on activity level:

* **Inactive or older dogs:**RER × 1.2 to 1.4
* **Average active dogs:**RER × 1.6
* **Very active or working dogs:**RER × 2.0 or more

If your dog is moderately active, multiply 1,170 by 1.6, which equals about 1,872 kcal per day.

Do you track your dog’s activity and weight regularly? Adjusting calories as they gain or lose weight is key to maintaining their ideal condition.

## Types Of Dog Food

Choosing the right food type is key for your 36 kg dog's health. Different foods offer various benefits. Each type affects how much you feed your dog daily. Understanding these types helps pick the best option.

### Dry Kibble Vs Wet Food

Dry kibble is popular and easy to store. It helps keep teeth clean by reducing plaque. Kibble is usually cost-effective and lasts longer once opened.

Wet food has higher moisture, which supports hydration. It often tastes better to dogs, encouraging picky eaters. Wet food is softer, making it easier to chew for older dogs.

Feeding amounts differ between these two types. Kibble is calorie-dense, so dogs eat less by weight. Wet food weighs more but has fewer calories per gram.

### Raw And Homemade Diets

Raw diets include uncooked meat, bones, and veggies. Many owners prefer raw food for its natural ingredients. It requires careful preparation to avoid harmful bacteria.

Homemade diets allow full control over ingredients. Owners can tailor meals to their dog's needs and allergies. Balancing nutrients is critical to avoid deficiencies or excesses.

Both raw and homemade diets often need supplements. Consulting a vet ensures your dog gets all essential nutrients. These diets usually require more time and effort than commercial foods.

## Grams Of Food Per Day

Knowing how many grams of food to give a 36 kg dog each day is key to keeping it healthy. Dogs need the right amount of food to stay active and maintain a good weight. Feeding too much or too little can cause health issues.

The daily grams of food depend on the dog's age, breed, and activity. This section explains the basic feeding guidelines and how to adjust portions based on activity levels.

### Standard Feeding Guidelines

For a 36 kg dog, the standard daily food amount is usually between 720 and 1080 grams. This range depends on the food type and its calorie content. Dry kibble and wet food have different calorie densities, so grams needed vary.

Most dog food packages have feeding charts. These charts suggest grams per day based on your dog’s weight. Use these as a starting point. Divide the total grams into two or three meals daily. This helps with digestion and avoids overeating.

### Adjusting Portions For Activity Level

Active dogs burn more calories. They need more food to fuel their energy. Increase daily grams by about 10% to 20% for dogs with high activity. For example, a working dog or one that runs daily.

Less active or older dogs need fewer calories. Reduce their daily grams to avoid weight gain. Watch your dog’s body shape and energy to find the right portion.

Adjust portions slowly. Monitor weight and health. Consult a vet for the best plan.

## Feeding Frequency

Feeding frequency plays a crucial role in managing your 36 kg dog’s health and energy levels. How often you feed can affect digestion, behavior, and even weight control. Finding the right routine depends on your dog’s age, activity, and individual needs.

### Meal Timing Options

You can choose from several meal timing options for your dog. Most owners prefer feeding twice a day—once in the morning and once in the evening. This schedule fits well with daily routines and helps regulate hunger.

Some dogs do better with three meals a day, especially if they are very active or have sensitive stomachs. Puppies and senior dogs often benefit from more frequent, smaller meals. You might also try feeding once a day, but this can lead to hunger and overeating, so it’s less common for larger dogs.

### Benefits Of Multiple Small Meals

Feeding your dog multiple small meals throughout the day can improve digestion and prevent bloating. Smaller portions reduce the risk of your dog eating too quickly and choking, which is common in bigger breeds.

Multiple meals also help keep your dog’s energy steady. Instead of big energy spikes and crashes, your dog gets a steady supply of nutrients. I noticed my own dog became calmer and more focused once I switched from two large meals to three smaller ones.

Consider your schedule and your dog’s preferences. Would feeding three times a day fit your routine? If yes, this could enhance your dog’s overall well-being and make mealtime less stressful for both of you.

## Monitoring And Adjusting Portions

Monitoring and adjusting your 36 kg dog’s food portions ensures they stay healthy and active. Dogs’ needs change with age, activity, and health. Careful observation helps provide the right amount of food daily. Overfeeding can cause weight gain. Underfeeding may lead to weakness or illness. Watch your dog closely to keep portions balanced.

### Recognizing Weight Changes

Look for signs of weight gain or loss regularly. Use a scale to check your dog’s weight every few weeks. Feel their ribs. You should feel ribs without pressing hard. If ribs become hard to find, your dog may be gaining too much weight. If ribs show too clearly, the dog might be losing weight.

Notice energy levels. A dog gaining weight may move less. Weight loss can cause tiredness or weakness. Keep a daily log of your dog’s weight and behavior. This helps spot changes early and adjust food amounts quickly.

### When To Modify Food Amounts

Change food portions based on weight trends and activity. Increase food if your dog loses weight or seems hungry. Decrease food if your dog gains weight or slows down. Puppies and active dogs may need more calories.

Consult your vet for advice if your dog’s weight changes rapidly. Avoid sudden large changes in food amount. Adjust portions slowly over several days. This keeps your dog’s digestion stable and avoids upset stomachs.

## Special Considerations

Feeding a 36 kg dog isn't as simple as measuring a fixed amount of food every day. Your dog's age, health, and lifestyle all play a big role in determining the right portion size. Understanding these special considerations helps you keep your dog healthy and happy.

### Puppies Vs Adult Dogs

Puppies need more calories per kilogram of body weight than adults because they are growing rapidly. A 36 kg puppy will require more grams of food daily compared to an adult dog of the same weight.

Since puppies burn energy faster, their meals should be split into multiple smaller servings throughout the day. This helps maintain steady energy levels and supports healthy digestion.

Have you noticed how your puppy’s appetite changes as they grow? Tracking these changes helps you adjust food amounts accurately.

### Senior Dog Feeding Tips

Older dogs often slow down and need fewer calories, but they still require balanced nutrition. Overfeeding can lead to weight gain, which stresses their joints and organs.

Look for senior dog formulas that include joint support and are easy to digest. Sometimes, adding wet food or mixing in supplements can improve their appetite and nutrient absorption.

Does your senior dog seem less interested in food? Smaller, more frequent meals might encourage better eating habits.

### Health Conditions Impacting Diet

Health problems like diabetes, kidney disease, or allergies can drastically change how much and what type of food your dog needs. Consulting your vet ensures you tailor the diet to manage these issues effectively.

For example, dogs with kidney problems often benefit from reduced protein and phosphorus in their food. Meanwhile, allergy-prone dogs might need hypoallergenic diets to avoid flare-ups.

Is your dog showing signs of discomfort or sudden weight changes? These might be clues to adjust their diet under professional guidance.
