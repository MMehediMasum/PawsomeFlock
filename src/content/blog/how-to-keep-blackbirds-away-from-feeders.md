## Why Blackbirds Visit Feeders

Blackbirds often visit feeders because they find easy food sources there. Their visits can be frequent and sometimes overwhelming. Understanding why blackbirds are attracted to feeders helps control their visits. It also helps protect other smaller birds that share your yard.

### Attraction To Food Types

Blackbirds prefer seeds that are large and easy to eat. They especially like sunflower seeds and cracked corn. These foods provide high energy and are easy to find in feeders. Blackbirds also enjoy suet and fruit, which some feeders offer. The availability of these foods encourages blackbirds to visit often.

Foods with a strong smell can attract blackbirds from a distance. They use their keen senses to find these food sources quickly. Avoiding certain food types can reduce blackbird visits to your feeder.

### Feeder Placement Issues

The location of your feeder plays a big role in attracting blackbirds. Feeders placed in open areas make blackbirds feel safe. They can easily spot predators and escape if needed. Blackbirds avoid feeders hidden by dense shrubs or trees.

Feeders near lawns or open fields also attract blackbirds. These areas offer easy landing spots and extra food on the ground. Placing feeders closer to bushes or trees can discourage blackbirds from visiting.

## Choosing The Right Feeder

Choosing the right feeder plays a key role in keeping blackbirds away from your bird feeding area. The type of feeder you use can either invite them in or discourage them from landing. By selecting feeders that don’t suit blackbirds’ feeding habits, you create a more welcoming space for the smaller birds you want to attract.

### Feeders Blackbirds Avoid

Blackbirds prefer feeders that offer open access to large quantities of seed. Avoid feeders that provide wide, open trays or large perches because these make it easy for blackbirds to land and feed comfortably.

Try using feeders designed for smaller birds, such as tube feeders with small feeding ports. These limit the size of birds that can feed, discouraging blackbirds from landing.

* Thistle or nyjer seed feeders attract finches and deter blackbirds.
* Hopper feeders with adjustable openings can be set to exclude larger birds.
* Platform feeders with wire mesh bottoms allow smaller birds to feed but can be challenging for blackbirds.

Have you noticed how blackbirds rarely visit feeders with narrow openings? That’s a clue to what they avoid.

### Designs That Limit Access

Feeders with designs that limit access to seeds can effectively keep blackbirds away. Look for feeders with small perches or no perches at all, forcing birds to hover or cling while feeding.

Weight-sensitive feeders that close access for heavier birds work well. Blackbirds are relatively heavy, so these feeders close off before they can feed.

| Feeder Design | How It Limits Blackbirds |
| --- | --- |
| Tube Feeders with Small Ports | Only small-billed birds can extract seeds |
| Weight-Activated Seed Ports | Shuts when heavy birds land |
| Feeders Without Perches | Requires hovering, which blackbirds avoid |

## Feed Selection Strategies

Choosing the right feed can help keep blackbirds away from your bird feeders. Blackbirds prefer certain seeds and avoid others. Using seeds they dislike reduces their visits. It also allows other birds to feed peacefully. Feed selection strategies focus on offering food that attracts small songbirds but deters blackbirds.

### Seeds Blackbirds Dislike

Blackbirds avoid small, hard seeds and shells. These seeds are less appealing to them but loved by smaller birds.

* Nyjer (thistle) seeds
* Millet
* Hulled sunflower seeds
* Safflower seeds

Nyjer seeds are tiny and hard to crack. Blackbirds prefer larger, softer seeds, so nyjer helps keep them away. Millet and safflower seeds are less tasty to blackbirds but attract finches and sparrows. Hulled sunflower seeds have no shells, making them less messy and less attractive to blackbirds.

### Alternative Food Options

Offering alternative foods can divert blackbirds from feeders. Provide food that suits other bird species but not blackbirds.

* Suet cakes with insect bits
* Fruit pieces like apple or grape slices
* Mealworms for insect-eating birds

Suet cakes attract woodpeckers and chickadees. Blackbirds find them less appealing. Fruits attract orioles and tanagers, not blackbirds. Mealworms are favorite snacks for bluebirds and wrens but ignored by blackbirds. These options help create a diverse feeding area while minimizing blackbird presence.

## Physical Deterrents For Blackbirds

Physical deterrents can be a straightforward and effective way to keep blackbirds away from your feeders without harming them. These methods create barriers or distractions that make the feeding area less inviting. You can try different options to see which works best for your space and the behavior of the birds around you.

### Using Spikes And Nets

Bird spikes are an easy-to-install option that prevents blackbirds from landing on feeder poles or nearby perches. They don’t hurt the birds but make it uncomfortable for them to settle. You can place spikes on flat surfaces around your feeders or on nearby ledges.

Bird nets create a physical barrier around the feeder, stopping blackbirds from accessing the food. They work well if you want to protect a specific feeder or fruit trees near the feeding area. Make sure the netting has small enough holes so blackbirds cannot squeeze through.

### Reflective And Moving Objects

Blackbirds dislike sudden movement and bright reflections. Hanging shiny objects like old CDs, aluminum foil strips, or reflective tape near your feeders can scare them away. The light flashes and movement disturb the birds enough to keep them at a distance.

Wind spinners or moving garden decorations also help by creating unpredictable motion. The key is to place these objects where they catch sunlight and create constant movement. Do you notice if blackbirds avoid areas where things flutter or shine?

  


## Behavioral Techniques

Behavioral techniques focus on changing how blackbirds interact with your feeders. These methods rely on altering feeding habits and surroundings. Small changes can reduce blackbird visits without harming the birds. Understanding their behavior helps create effective strategies that protect your feeder and welcome other bird species.

### Changing Feeding Times

Blackbirds prefer certain times to feed, often early morning or late afternoon. Feeding at different times can reduce their presence. Try offering food during midday when blackbirds are less active. This simple shift can make your feeder less attractive to them.

Keep a consistent schedule once you find a time that works. Birds get used to patterns, so sticking to new times keeps blackbirds away. This technique also helps other birds feel safe to visit.

### Creating Distractions

Providing alternative food sources can distract blackbirds from your main feeder. Scatter seeds or fruits away from the feeder area. This draws their attention elsewhere and reduces crowding.

Use decoys or shiny objects nearby to catch blackbirds’ eyes. Moving items like pinwheels or reflective tape can startle and deter them. Change these distractions regularly to keep blackbirds guessing.

## Maintaining A Blackbird-free Feeder

Keeping blackbirds away from your feeders requires ongoing effort and attention. A feeder free of blackbirds not only attracts the smaller songbirds you want but also prevents the mess and damage these larger birds can cause. To maintain a blackbird-free feeder, it’s essential to focus on cleanliness and adaptability in your approach.

### Regular Cleaning Tips

Cleaning your feeder frequently helps reduce leftover seeds that attract blackbirds. Wet, moldy, or spilled seed often draws unwanted visitors more than fresh food does. Make a habit of washing your feeder with warm, soapy water at least once a week.

Check for seed buildup around the feeding area and clear it away daily if possible. This removes the easy food source blackbirds love. You might be surprised how much difference a clean feeder makes in keeping blackbirds at bay.

### Monitoring And Adjusting Methods

Keep an eye on which birds visit your feeder and how they behave. If you notice blackbirds returning, try changing the seed type or adjusting feeder placement. Moving feeders closer to shrubs or trees with dense cover can discourage blackbirds, who prefer open spaces.

Don’t hesitate to experiment with different strategies over time. Have you tried switching from sunflower seeds to safflower seeds? Blackbirds generally avoid safflower, and you may find this simple change reduces their visits. Adjustments based on what you observe can keep your feeder welcoming to the birds you enjoy most.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How Can I Prevent Blackbirds From Eating Feeder Food?

Use feeders with small openings designed for smaller birds. Place feeders away from open spaces where blackbirds gather. Also, try offering foods that blackbirds dislike, such as safflower seeds, to reduce their attraction.

### What Types Of Feeders Deter Blackbirds Effectively?

Tube feeders with narrow ports work well to keep blackbirds out. Hopper feeders with weight-sensitive perches can also exclude larger birds. These designs restrict access to only smaller songbirds, keeping blackbirds away.

### Are There Natural Repellents To Keep Blackbirds Away?

Yes, using reflective tape or shiny objects near feeders can scare blackbirds. Hanging predator decoys like owls or hawks also helps. These natural deterrents make blackbirds feel unsafe and discourage feeding.

### Does Feeder Placement Affect Blackbird Visits?

Absolutely. Place feeders near shrubs or trees that small birds use for cover. Avoid open, sunny areas where blackbirds feel comfortable. Strategic placement reduces blackbird presence at your feeders.
